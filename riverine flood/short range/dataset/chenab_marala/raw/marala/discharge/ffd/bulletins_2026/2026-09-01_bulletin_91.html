RIVERS SITUATION AND QUALITATIVE FLOOD FORECAST

I. METEOROLOGICAL FEATURES 

  • Yesterday's westerly wave over northeast Afghanistan persists and its trough is affecting northern parts of the country.

  • Seasonal low-pressure area lies over northeast Balochistan.

  • Yesterday's low pressure system over north Bay of Bengal and adjoining northeast Orrisa (India) today lies over west West Bengal (India).

  • Moist currents from Arabian sea are penetrating into the upper parts of the country up to 3,000 feet.

II. METEOROLOGICAL OUTLOOK              

  • Scattered to widespread rain-wind/thunderstorm is expected over the upper catchments of  all the major rivers, Islamabad, Kashmir, upper Punjab and Upper Khyber Pakhtunkhwa during next 36-hours. However, isolated heavy fall is also expected over Islamabad, Kashmir, along with upper catchments of Indus, Jhelum and Chenab rivers during the period.

III. HYDROLOGICAL SITUATION at 0600 PST

  • Tarbela reservoir is currently at its Maximum Conservation Level, while Mangla reservoir is 77% filled.  

  • All major rivers are flowing at normal levels.

 

IV. HYDROLOGICAL OUTLOOK

  • River Indus at Kalabagh and Chashma is expected to attain Medium Level Flood during next 24-36 hours.

  • Flows in rivers Jhelum and Chenab and their associated tributaries are expected to increase moderately during next 48-hours.

  • Widespread rain-wind/thunderstorm with isolated heavy fall is expected over the upper catchments of Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi and Sutlej rivers, Islamabad, Kashmir,

    Khyber Pakhtunkhwa: Abbottabad, Buner, Charsadda, Haripur, Kohat, Malakand, Mansehra, Mardan, Nowshera, Shangla, Swabi, Swat and Tor Ghar. 

    Punjab: Attock, Chakwal, Gujrat, Jhelum, Narowal, Rawalpindi and Sialkot.

  • Scattered rain-wind/thunderstorm is expected over the upper catchments of Indus river,

    Khyber Pakhtunkhwa: Bajaur, Bannu, Batagram, Hangu, Karak, Khyber, Kohistan, Kurram, Lakki Marwat, Mohmand, North Waziristan, Orakzai, Peshawar and Dir. 

    Punjab: Gujranwala, Hafizabad, Khushab, Lahore, Mandi Bahauddin, Mianwali, Sargodha and Sheikhupura. 

    Gilgit-Baltistan: Astore, Darel, Diamir, Ghizer, Gilgit, Gupis, Hunza, Nagar, Rondu, Shigar and Tangir.

  • Mainly hot and humid weather is expected over remaining most parts of the country. 

VI. WEATHER OUTLOOK FOR NEXT 48-HOURS   

  • Scattered rain-wind/thunderstorm is expected over the upper catchments of Chenab, Ravi and Sutlej rivers, Islamabad, Kashmir, However, isolated heavy fall is also expected over the upper catchments of Chenab and Ravi rivers.

    Khyber Pakhtunkhwa: Abbottabad, Bannu, Buner, Charsadda, Haripur, Karak, Kohat, Lakki Marwat, Mansehra, Mardan, Nowshera, Peshawar, Swabi, Tank and Tor Ghar. 

    Punjab: Attock, Chakwal, Gujranwala, Gujrat, Hafizabad, Jhelum, Lahore, Mandi Bahauddin, Mianwali, Narowal, Rawalpindi, Sargodha, Sheikhupura and Sialkot. 

  • Isolated rain-wind/thunderstorm is expected over the upper catchments of Indus and Jhelum rivers.

  • Mainly hot and humid weather is expected over remaining most parts of the country.

5. HOW CLOSE IS EACH RIVER TO ITS LIMIT?

Kalabagh is in low to medium flood at 255.1 — 119.9 below its medium flood level (375.0). Each bar is one station’s own flood levels across its maximum capacity; the solid run is the flow so far, the pale run is the room still left.

All figures in thousands of cusecs. Full bar = maximum (design) capacity. Solid colour is the flow reached so far and the pale wash beside it is the room still left; the dark line between them is the current reading, printed beneath it. Thin white lines are that station’s flood levels with their thresholds beneath, so the same colour can begin at a different point on every bar. Each band is named inside itself — initialled (BL, L, M, H, VH, EH) where the band is too narrow for the words, and left blank where it is too narrow for those. Stations without a design capacity are not shown.
