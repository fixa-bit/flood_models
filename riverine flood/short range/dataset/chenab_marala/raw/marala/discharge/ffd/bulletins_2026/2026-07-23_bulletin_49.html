RAINFALL FORECAST — NEXT 24 & 48 HOURS

1. RIVERS SITUATION AND QUALITATIVE FLOOD FORECAST

I: HYDROLOGICAL SITUATION at 0600 PST AND OUTLOOK

  • River Chenab at Khanki and Qadirabad is in High flood level. River Chenab at Marala is in Medium flood level. River Indus at Tarbela and Chashma, river Kabul at Nowshera, river Chenab at Chiniot Bridge and river Ravi at Balloki are in Low flood level. All other major rivers are flowing in their normal flows at other gauging stations.

 

  • WARNING: In the presence of a westerly weather system coupled with strong monsoon deflected currents from the Bay of Bengal, Scattered to widespread rain-wind/thunderstorm with isolated heavy to very heavy falls is expected over the catchments of all major rivers, including upper Punjab and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. Consequently, river flows are likely to increase significantly upto 25th July.

    • Medium to High flows are also expected in river Kabul and its associated tributaries.

      • Warsak and Munda Dam  in Kabul river basin is expected to be in low to medium level flood within next 24-hours.

      • Currently High to medium level flows in river Chenab is expected to decrease in next 24-hours.

      • Flash flooding is expected in the hill torrents of DG Khan, the streams of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and northern Balochistan.

      • Urban Flooding is also expected in Rawalpindi, Lahore, Gujranwala, Mandi Bahauddin, Sialkot, Narowal, Sargodha, Faisalabad, Okara and Sahiwal.

II: METEOROLOGICAL FEATURES

  • Yesterday’s trough of westerly wave over northern parts of the country persists.

  • Monsoon trough extends from central parts of the country passes through northern parts of India along the foot hills of Himalaya.

  • Yesterday's low-pressure area over southeast Rajasthan (India) today lies over south Rajasthan and it is expected to move south westwards over northeast Gujarat (India) within next 24-hours. 

  • The seasonal low-pressure area lies over Northwest Balochistan.

  • Moderate moist currents from Bay of Bengal are penetrating into the upper parts of the country up to 7,000 feet.

5. HOW CLOSE IS EACH RIVER TO ITS LIMIT?

Qadirabad is in medium to high flood at 245.2 — 154.8 below its very high flood level (400.0). Each bar is one station’s own flood levels across its maximum capacity; the solid run is the flow so far, the pale run is the room still left.

All figures in thousands of cusecs. Full bar = maximum (design) capacity. Solid colour is the flow reached so far and the pale wash beside it is the room still left; the dark line between them is the current reading, printed beneath it. Thin white lines are that station’s flood levels with their thresholds beneath, so the same colour can begin at a different point on every bar. Each band is named inside itself — initialled (BL, L, M, H, VH, EH) where the band is too narrow for the words, and left blank where it is too narrow for those. Stations without a design capacity are not shown.
