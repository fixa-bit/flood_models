RAINFALL FORECAST — NEXT 24 & 48 HOURS

1. RIVERS SITUATION AND QUALITATIVE FLOOD FORECAST

I: HYDROLOGICAL SITUATION at 0600 PST AND OUTLOOK

  • River Indus at Kalabagh, Taunsa, Guddu and Sukkur are in Low flood level at downstream.

  • River Jhelum at Mangla is in Low flood level at upstream.

  • River Ravi at Balloki & Sidhnai are in Low flood level at upstream.

  • River Indus at Guddu has attained Medium flood level at upstream. Whereas, Sukkur will remain in Low flood level during next 24-hours, after that it will also attain medium flood level at upstream. 

     

  • WARNING: In the presence of westerly weather system coupled with monsoon currents from Arabian sea and Bay of Bengal, widespread rain-wind/thunderstorm with isolated heavy to very heavy falls is expected upto 04th August, consequently,

    • River Chenab at Marala, Khanki and Qadirabad is expected to fluctuate between Low to Medium flood level.

    • Medium to High level flows are expected in nullahs of Jhelum, Chenab (Dora, Dotta, Dowara, Bhimber, Halsi, Aik and Palkhu) and Ravi (Bein, Bassanter, Deg and Sakki). 

    • Flash flooding is expected in the hill torrents of DG Khan, the streams of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and northern Balochistan.

    • Urban Flooding is also expected in Rawalpindi, Jhelum, Gujranwala, Sargodha, Mandi Bahauddin, Sialkot, Gujrat, Narowal, Lahore, Okara, Sahiwal and Faisalabad. 

       

II: METEOROLOGICAL FEATURES

  • Yesterday's trough of westerly wave over northeast Afghanistan today lies over northern part of the country.

  • Yesterday’s Deep Depression over northeast Orissa today lies over north east Chattisgarh (India) with central pressure (992 hPa). It is likely to move westwards over northeast Madhya Pradesh (India).

  • A vertically elongated seasonal low-pressure area with central pressure lies over Northeast Balochistan.

  • Moderate moist currents from Arabian sea and weak moist current from Bay of Bengal (along the foot hills of Himalayan mountains) are penetrating into upper parts of the country up to 5,000 feet.

2. WEEKLY WEATHER OUTLOOK FOR RIVER’S CATCHMENTS

(30th- July, 2026 to 5th- August, 2026)

RAINFALL OUTLOOK:

  • The current wet spell is likely to intensify from 30th July in upper catchments of all the major rivers,  and Sindh is likely to persist uptill 04th August, 2026.

  • WARNING: On account of upcoming strong monsoon incursions from both Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal, fairly widespread to widespread rain-wind/thunderstorm with isolated heavy to very heavy falls is expected over Islamabad, Punjab, Kashmir, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Gilgit Baltistan and southeast Sindh. Consequently, river flows are likely to increase in significantly in all major rivers upto 04th August.

    • Medium to High level flows are expected in nullahs of Jhelum, Chenab (Dora, Dotta, Dowara, Bhimber, Halsi, Aik and Palkhu) and Ravi (Bein, Bassanter, Deg and Sakki). 

    • Flash flooding is expected in the hill torrents of DG Khan, the streams of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and northern Balochistan.

5. HOW CLOSE IS EACH RIVER TO ITS LIMIT?

Guddu is in medium flood at 383.7 — 116.3 below its high flood level (500.0). Each bar is one station’s own flood levels across its maximum capacity; the solid run is the flow so far, the pale run is the room still left.

All figures in thousands of cusecs. Full bar = maximum (design) capacity. Solid colour is the flow reached so far and the pale wash beside it is the room still left; the dark line between them is the current reading, printed beneath it. Thin white lines are that station’s flood levels with their thresholds beneath, so the same colour can begin at a different point on every bar. Each band is named inside itself — initialled (BL, L, M, H, VH, EH) where the band is too narrow for the words, and left blank where it is too narrow for those. Stations without a design capacity are not shown.
