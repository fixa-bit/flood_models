Read this first
These are retrospective tests using observed Marala discharge and, in many experiments, reanalysis weather as a stand-in for future forecasts. The 30-day tests do not prove real-time 30-day forecast skill. “Flood” in the comparisons means flow above a training-data 95th-percentile threshold; it is not an official warning category.

Prepared from the model folders and evaluation reports saved in this project. Scores are transcribed from those reports; no models were retrained for these notes.

How to read the scores

Flow error

RMSE is the typical size of forecast errors, with extra penalty for big misses. Lower is better. It is measured here in cubic metres per second (m³/s).

NSE compares a forecast with a simple average-flow reference. 1 is perfect; 0 is about as good as that reference; below 0 is worse. It is not a percent accuracy score.

Flood detection

Recall asks: of the observed high-flow days, how many did the model catch? Precision asks: of the days it warned, how many were high flow?

There are far fewer high-flow days than ordinary-flow days. So overall accuracy can look high even when many floods are missed. Check flood recall and flood RMSE.

Important limit: most weather-driven tests use realized ERA5-Land weather with added noise, not archived weather forecasts that would have been available on the forecast date. Treat the scores as model comparisons under a weather proxy, not operational warning accuracy. The test years (mainly 2022–2024) have also been viewed in earlier project work.

Forecast picture

“Lead 10” means the flow forecast for ten days after the issue date. Leads are evaluated against observed discharge only; missing targets are excluded.

Section 1 · Model families and why we tried them

We tested simple references first, then machine-learning, transfer-learning, and hydrology-based approaches. The purpose is to see what adds value for Marala, where local observed history is limited and floods are uncommon.

15-day

The directly comparable fine-tuned vs scratch 15-day encoder–decoder evaluation used the same 2022–2024 benchmark and 51 flood days. At lead 15: fine-tuned flood RMSE 633 m³/s vs scratch 680; flood recall at lead 10 was 0.804 for both. Ordinary-day NSE at lead 10 was 0.762 (fine-tuned) and 0.765 (scratch). Fine-tuning helped the flood error slightly, not every metric.

Simple takeaway: for average flow at 30 days, the residual ensemble scored highest in the saved table. For catching 30-day high flows, HBV + LSTM had the highest recall, but it also had a high false-alarm ratio (about 0.56). For the short 10-day task, the 15-day fine-tuned sequence model and 8-basin fine-tuning show promising flood errors/recall, but these need fresh operational-forecast tests.

Observed test

Score forecasts only when the target discharge was observed.

Many LSTM models used up to 365 previous days and predicted 10, 15, or 30 future days. The newer local neural models mask missing discharge targets rather than filling them. Input gaps may be handled using training-period means and missing-value flags. Weather “forecast” inputs in most experiments were realized reanalysis with added noise because archived 10–30-day weather forecasts were not found.

Data limits: local discharge covers about nine years in the core model table, high-flow test days are scarce, and flow is affected by river regulation. The chosen high-flow threshold is a training-data Q95 proxy. More independently held-out years, additional basins, official flood labels, and archived forecast weather would make future evaluations more convincing.

What not to conclude

  • NSE is a skill score, not “percent accurate.”
  • Overall accuracy can hide missed floods because ordinary-flow days dominate.
  • The high-flow cutoff is a statistical proxy, not a government warning level.
  • One small test sample cannot prove that a model will work in future flood seasons.
  • Realized reanalysis with artificial noise is not the same as weather information available at forecast time.
  • Scores are only directly comparable when dates, targets, lead, threshold and evaluation method match.
  • The 30-day fine-tuned encoder result was adjusted using validation information; its interval coverage is not an independent guarantee.
