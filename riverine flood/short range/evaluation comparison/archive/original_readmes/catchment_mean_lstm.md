# Spatially distributed model: LSTM per elevation band

This experiment retains the spatial distribution of rainfall, temperature and
snow by processing four real elevation-band histories independently. It uses one
hindcast LSTM and forecast LSTM per band, then joins their area-weighted hidden
features into a non-crossing quantile discharge head.

## Sources and spatial meaning

Fresh data comes from [ERA5-Land daily grids](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/ECMWF_ERA5_LAND_DAILY_AGGR)
through the existing Earth Engine connection. [MERIT DEM](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/MERIT_DEM_v1_0_3)
defines `<1500`, `1500-<3000`, `3000-4500` and `>4500` metre bands inside the
project's existing Marala catchment polygon. Approximate area fractions are
0.2450, 0.2242, 0.2835 and 0.2472. Each band has its own daily precipitation,
temperature, SWE and snowmelt, extracted from the actual weather pixels and
weighted by band area within each pixel. These are not basin-average values
duplicated four times or generated by an assumed temperature lapse rate.

The DEM is about 90 m; weather stays at native ERA5-Land resolution, about 11 km.
Elevation masks aggregate information from those weather cells. They do not
create 90 m weather observations. This is a semi-distributed elevation-band
model, with no explicit river network or hillslope routing.

The extraction covers 2015-2024, providing 365 days of context for the observed
Marala experiment beginning in 2016. Precipitation and SWE convert metres to mm,
temperature converts Kelvin to degrees C, and snowmelt converts metres/day to
mm/day. Daily means are UTC; accumulated variables are the source's daily sums.
Full band coverage is required before a daily value is accepted. Raw CSVs,
metadata and SHA256 receipts are saved locally.

The old NASA POWER grid is flagged HOLD and is excluded. An initial extraction
failed coverage checks; its incomplete files are also excluded. Only the active
`data/raw/era5land_elevation_bands_v2` folder is read by the current config.
The catchment polygon is a reviewed research delineation, not an agency-certified
boundary. Retain its original attribution/terms, including Global Watersheds,
MERIT Hydro and MERIT Basins. ERA5-Land and MERIT terms are linked on their
dataset pages. Snow data are reanalysis estimates, not new ground measurements.

## Model and comparison

The distributed model uses 32 hidden units per band, four independent encoder
and forecast pairs, 365 historical days and 10 future days. Each band receives
its own historical rain, temperature, SWE and melt plus missingness/calendar
channels. Future inputs are precipitation and temperature per band, plus lead
embeddings. No future SWE or observed future flow enters a model input.
Area-weighted latent features retain band identity until the output head.

The control is a conventional 64-unit LSTM trained on area-weighted catchment
means of exactly the same four variables. Forecast errors are generated in
physical units and shared across the bands. The control averages those same
noisy band forecasts, so a noise-realization difference cannot masquerade as a
spatial benefit. Precipitation is clipped to zero inside forecast samples after
noise, before both models receive it. Source files are unchanged. This shared
spatial error assumption is simplified; neither model uses a real forecast archive.

Both models use train years 2016-2020, validation 2021 and test 2022-2024, seed 42,
masked pinball loss, training-only normalization, early stopping and quantiles
0.05/0.25/0.5/0.75/0.95. Missing targets stay NaN and are excluded individually.
No observed discharge is filled or modified. The original observed table remains
at `../data/marala_daily.csv`; preparation creates a separate table preserving
its target values and NaN positions exactly.

The architectures have different parameter counts, reported with the results.
A single comparison cannot prove that all performance differences come from
retaining spatial information. The original eight-input LSTM and ensemble are
also shown for context; their sources/features/noise differ from this experiment.

## Run and retrain

Run from the parent `model` folder. Extraction uses the already authorized Earth
Engine environment; training uses the existing PyTorch environment:

```powershell
$python = "../experiments/marala_observed/.venv/Scripts/python.exe"
$eePython = "../dataset/chenab_marala/raw/marala/rainfall/google_earth_engine/.venv/Scripts/python.exe"
& $eePython spatially_distributed_model/download_data.py --config spatially_distributed_model/extraction.json
& $python spatially_distributed_model/prepare_data.py --config spatially_distributed_model/config.yaml
& $python spatially_distributed_model/train.py --config spatially_distributed_model/config.yaml
& $python spatially_distributed_model/evaluate.py --config spatially_distributed_model/config.yaml
```

`run_pipeline.py` runs those stages in order and stops when any stage fails.
Preparation and training refuse to overwrite existing datasets/checkpoints.
Choose new prepared/output paths before another experiment. Downloads can resume
verified source years. Source configurations are plain JSON/YAML. A different
catchment can be supplied before extraction; compatible band inputs use the same
model code. Keep the two stage configs and band definitions consistent.

```powershell
& $python -m unittest discover -s spatially_distributed_model/tests -v
& $python -m unittest discover -s tests -v
& $python -m unittest discover -s hybrid_physics_ml/tests -v
```

The 6 spatial tests verify shapes/alignment, distinct band influence, no future
leak into historical states, paired physical forecast errors, missing-band flags,
unchanged target files and checkpoint training/loading. The 20 existing tests
cover shared training and the earlier hybrid module. Synthetic tests check code
correctness only; they are not measured streamflow accuracy.

## Limits

Archived spatial weather forecasts remain unavailable. No operational forecast
skill is claimed from realized future reanalysis. The short discharge record,
regulation/diversions, limited independent extremes and overlapping target windows
limit interpretation. These elevation bands discard within-band geography and do
not resolve glacier energy balance, lake/river routing or canal operations.
Weather publication latency and discharge reading time need operational review.
The test years were previously inspected in workspace experiments: held out from
this fitting run, but not an untouched project-wide holdout.
